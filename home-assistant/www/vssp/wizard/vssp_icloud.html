1 iCloud account

Use the real Apple ID password. Apple's app-specific passwords only open Mail and Calendar — they cannot reach the photo library. It is written to a protected file on the instance, never passed on a command line.

2 Verification code

Apple sent a six-digit code to your trusted devices. This is the one step no form can take for you. Once validated, the session is trusted for about two months and nothing here will ask again.

No code arriving? Press FORGET THE SESSION, then SAVE & CONNECT again — a stored session can report a linked account without ever asking Apple for anything.

3 Destination

Where the files land. A partition or a NAS share added in Settings > System > Storage appears under /media and needs nothing else. The host path is only used to grow the volume, and only applies to a local partition — a NAS is grown on the NAS.

Only growing is offered. Shrinking a mounted filesystem destroys data when it goes wrong and cannot be undone from a dashboard.
4 The library

Scanning reads every item in iCloud into a local index — no photo is downloaded. It is what the counters and the picker below are built from. On a large library the first pass takes a while and keeps reporting as it goes.

5 Choose what to keep

Tick what should move to your own storage. A green tick means the item is already stored here. Only an item verified on disk can then be removed from iCloud.

Thumbnails are cached under /local, which Home Assistant serves without authentication. Their names are unguessable and a transferred item's thumbnail is erased at once — use ERASE THUMBNAILS to clear the rest.
6 Or transfer everything

Hands the whole job to icloudpd, which downloads everything not already on disk and resumes where it stopped. Switch on FREE iCLOUD in the console to have it also remove, from iCloud, what it has just stored — keeping the most recent days.
